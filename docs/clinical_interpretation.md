# Clinical Interpretation of CYP2C19*2 in Clopidogrel Therapy

## 1. Introduction

Clopidogrel is an antiplatelet prodrug that requires metabolic activation to produce its therapeutic effect. The CYP2C19 enzyme contributes significantly to this activation process.

Genetic variation in CYP2C19 can influence enzyme activity and consequently affect the amount of active clopidogrel metabolite produced. Among the clinically important variants, CYP2C19*2 (rs4244285) is a no-function allele associated with reduced CYP2C19 activity.

Reduced clopidogrel activation may result in inadequate platelet inhibition and an increased risk of adverse cardiovascular events in susceptible patients.

This document discusses the clinical evidence supporting the CYP2C19–clopidogrel interaction and the genotype-guided prescribing recommendations provided by the Clinical Pharmacogenetics Implementation Consortium (CPIC).

## 2. Relationship Between CYP2C19 Genotype and Clopidogrel Response

CYP2C19 metabolizer phenotype influences the capacity of an individual to activate clopidogrel.

Individuals carrying CYP2C19 loss-of-function alleles may produce lower concentrations of the active metabolite compared with normal metabolizers.

The resulting reduction in platelet inhibition can increase on-treatment platelet reactivity and may contribute to adverse ischemic outcomes.

The CYP2C19*2 allele is classified as a no-function allele. Its clinical effect depends on the combination of CYP2C19 alleles inherited from both parents.

### Examples of CYP2C19 diplotypes

| Diplotype | Expected metabolizer phenotype | Functional interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| *1/*1 | Normal metabolizer | Normal CYP2C19 activity |
| *1/*2 | Intermediate metabolizer | Reduced CYP2C19 activity |
| *2/*2 | Poor metabolizer | Significantly reduced or absent CYP2C19 activity |

These examples illustrate common diplotypes involving CYP2C19*2. Complete phenotype assignment requires consideration of both alleles and their functional classifications.

## 3. Clinical Evidence: Mega et al. (2009)

Mega et al. (2009) investigated the relationship between cytochrome P450 genetic polymorphisms and the response to clopidogrel.

The study demonstrated that patients carrying CYP2C19 reduced-function alleles had lower exposure to clopidogrel's active metabolite and reduced platelet inhibition compared with non-carriers.

### 3.1 Cardiovascular Outcomes

Among clopidogrel-treated patients, carriers of CYP2C19 reduced-function alleles experienced a higher rate of the composite primary cardiovascular outcome compared with non-carriers.

| Clinical outcome | Reduced-function allele carriers | Non-carriers |
|---|---:|---:|
| Primary composite cardiovascular outcome | 12.1% | 8.0% |
| Definite or probable stent thrombosis | 2.6% | 0.8% |

For the primary composite cardiovascular outcome, the hazard ratio was 1.53 (95% CI: 1.07–2.19).

For definite or probable stent thrombosis, the hazard ratio was 3.09 (95% CI: 1.19–8.00).

### 3.2 Findings Specific to CYP2C19*2

The CYP2C19*2 allele accounted for approximately 95% of the reduced-function CYP2C19 allele carriers in the study.

In the CYP2C19*2 carrier analysis, the primary cardiovascular outcome occurred in 11.7% of carriers compared with 8.3% of non-carriers.

The reported hazard ratio was 1.42 (95% CI: 0.98–2.05).

For stent thrombosis, the corresponding rates were 2.7% in carriers and 0.8% in non-carriers, with a hazard ratio of 3.33 (95% CI: 1.28–8.62).

These findings support the clinical importance of CYP2C19 genetic variation in determining clopidogrel response.

**Interpretation:** The main cardiovascular outcome comparisons of 12.1% versus 8.0% and 2.6% versus 0.8% refer to carriers of reduced-function CYP2C19 alleles as a group. They should not be presented as results exclusively involving CYP2C19*2 carriers.

## 4. CPIC Guidelines for CYP2C19 and Clopidogrel Therapy

The Clinical Pharmacogenetics Implementation Consortium (CPIC) provides evidence-based recommendations for interpreting CYP2C19 genotype results when considering clopidogrel therapy.

The 2022 CPIC guideline explains how metabolizer phenotype influences clopidogrel activation and provides recommendations according to the patient's clinical indication.

The recommendations are particularly important for patients with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) and those undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI).

### 4.1 Recommendations for ACS and/or PCI

| CYP2C19 phenotype | Effect on clopidogrel metabolism | CPIC recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Ultrarapid metabolizer (UM) | Increased active metabolite formation | Standard-dose clopidogrel (75 mg/day) if prescribed |
| Rapid metabolizer (RM) | Normal or increased active metabolite formation | Standard-dose clopidogrel (75 mg/day) if prescribed |
| Normal metabolizer (NM) | Normal active metabolite formation | Standard-dose clopidogrel (75 mg/day) |
| Intermediate metabolizer (IM) | Reduced active metabolite formation | Avoid standard-dose clopidogrel if possible; use an alternative P2Y12 inhibitor |
| Poor metabolizer (PM) | Significantly reduced active metabolite formation | Avoid clopidogrel if possible; use an alternative P2Y12 inhibitor |

For intermediate and poor metabolizers in ACS and/or PCI settings, CPIC strongly recommends considering an alternative P2Y12 inhibitor at the standard recommended dose when no contraindication exists.

The preferred alternatives include:

**Prasugrel**

Prasugrel is an alternative P2Y12 receptor inhibitor. It can be considered in patients who are intermediate or poor metabolizers when clinically appropriate. However, prasugrel is contraindicated in patients with a history of stroke or transient ischemic attack (TIA).

**Ticagrelor**

Ticagrelor is another P2Y12 receptor inhibitor. Unlike clopidogrel, it does not require CYP2C19-mediated metabolic activation to exert its antiplatelet effect. It may be considered when clinically appropriate and when there are no contraindications.

### 4.2 Recommendations for Other Cardiovascular Indications

For cardiovascular indications outside ACS and PCI, CPIC recommends standard-dose clopidogrel (75 mg/day) for normal metabolizers.

For poor metabolizers, clopidogrel should be avoided if possible, and an alternative such as prasugrel or ticagrelor may be considered if there are no contraindications.

The guideline does not provide a recommendation for intermediate, rapid or ultrarapid metabolizers in this particular non-ACS, non-PCI cardiovascular setting because of insufficient evidence.

### 4.3 Recommendations for Neurovascular Indications

For neurovascular indications, including acute ischemic stroke and transient ischemic attack, CPIC recommends standard-dose clopidogrel for normal metabolizers.

For intermediate and poor metabolizers, an alternative P2Y12 inhibitor may be considered at the standard dose if clinically indicated and if no contraindication exists.

Prasugrel is contraindicated in patients with a history of stroke or TIA. Therefore, treatment selection requires careful consideration of the patient's clinical condition.

## 5. Clinical Significance of CYP2C19*2

The CYP2C19*2 allele has important pharmacogenomic implications because it reduces the capacity of the CYP2C19 enzyme to contribute to clopidogrel activation.

Individuals with a *1/*2 diplotype are generally classified as intermediate metabolizers, whereas individuals with a *2/*2 diplotype are generally classified as poor metabolizers.

In patients with ACS and/or undergoing PCI, these phenotypes are associated with reduced active metabolite formation and increased on-treatment platelet reactivity.

The CPIC guideline therefore recommends avoiding standard-dose clopidogrel in intermediate metabolizers and avoiding clopidogrel where possible in poor metabolizers in these clinical settings.

Importantly, CPIC does not recommend routinely increasing the clopidogrel dose to overcome reduced CYP2C19 function. Alternative antiplatelet therapy should be considered according to metabolizer phenotype, clinical indication and contraindications.

## 6. Relevance to Population Pharmacogenomics

Population-level variation in CYP2C19*2 frequency provides useful context for pharmacogenomic research.

The population analysis in this project identified differences in CYP2C19*2 allele frequency across broad ancestry groups and selected South Asian and HGDP populations.

However, a higher allele frequency in a population does not automatically establish a higher rate of inadequate clopidogrel response.

Clinical response depends on the complete CYP2C19 diplotype, other genetic factors, environmental influences, treatment characteristics and the patient's clinical condition.

Therefore, population-frequency findings should be interpreted alongside clinical pharmacogenomic evidence rather than used as direct predictors of individual treatment outcomes.

## 7. Important Clinical Considerations

- CYP2C19*2 is a no-function allele, but the presence of one copy does not automatically establish poor metabolizer status.
- Metabolizer phenotype must be assigned using the complete diplotype.
- CPIC guidelines explain how available genetic results can guide medication selection; they do not mandate universal CYP2C19 testing.
- Alternative antiplatelet drugs must be selected according to clinical indication and contraindications.
- Pharmacogenomic recommendations do not replace individual medical assessment.
